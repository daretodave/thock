Wuque Studio published a product-update post to its blog on July 26, 2026 — the kind of periodic status note vendors use to touch base with a subscriber list between launches, not a single splashy reveal. It didn't read as headline news on its own. What it did was put the vendor's name back in front of buyers at a moment when interest in one of its older boards was already climbing quietly, and the two effects have compounded through the weeks since.

## The post itself

Wuque Studio's July 26 entry is a general product-update — the studio checking in on where its lines stand rather than announcing a specific launch. Nothing in it names a new group buy, a Mammoth follow-up, or a Promise revision. That's worth stating plainly rather than reading between the lines: the source material supports a status update and nothing more specific than that. Readers who saw the post circulate and assumed a new board announcement was imminent are ahead of what Wuque itself has actually said.

That gap between what circulated and what was confirmed is the whole story here, and it's a familiar shape. A vendor with an established back catalog posts something, the post gets shared past the people who read it carefully, and the resulting search spike attaches itself to whichever of the vendor's older boards already has the most cultural residue. In Wuque's case, that board is the Ikki68 Aurora.

## The board it revived

The <PartReference id="ikki68-aurora">Ikki68 Aurora</PartReference> is not a new board. Wuque Studio has sold it since 2021, and it has spent the years since as one of the most-cited reference points in the enthusiast market for what a polycarbonate case actually sounds like — a frosted, translucent 65% shell with a gasket mount and per-key RGB that diffuses softly through the case walls rather than punching through a solid one. thock's own [case-materials-compared](/article/case-materials-compared) guide leans on it directly, calling it the canonical example of a PC case reading "acoustically brighter and airier than most aluminium boards at similar or lower price points." A board doesn't earn that kind of standing reference status by accident; it earns it by staying in stock, staying relevant, and giving reviewers and forum posters a consistent, executable example to point new buyers toward for half a decade running.

That's precisely the profile of board a stray vendor mention reactivates. The Ikki68 Aurora didn't change — no new SKU, no revised colorway, no confirmed hardware update accompanies this resurgence. What changed is visibility: a listing that's been quietly available got pulled back into active searches and shopping-cart consideration because Wuque's name was freshly in front of people. That's a real signal worth naming, and it's also a modest one. Renewed interest in an in-stock board is not the same event as a new product launch, even when the two get talked about in the same threads.

Wuque's other flagship line offers a useful contrast on exactly this point. The <PartReference id="mammoth75">Mammoth75</PartReference> — the studio's 75% with the exploded arrow cluster and the magnetic knob that thock covered as part of the [75% layout's rise to default status](/article/75-percent-default) — has an actual, confirmed group-buy history behind it. That's the shape a real Wuque product cycle takes: an open buy, a stated delivery window, a listed configuration. Nothing about the current summer-update chatter has produced that shape yet for anything new.

The same contrast holds one vendor over. Mode's [Sonnet R2](/article/mode-sonnet-r2-group-buy-coverage), another 65% board, ran a full group buy at CannonKeys from June through mid-July — an open window, a stated timeline, an actual close date — while the Ikki68 Aurora sits in the same size class with no buy at all, just a decade of steady in-stock availability. Two different ways for a 65% board to stay relevant, and worth keeping straight when a vendor post reactivates interest in one of them.

## Confirmed versus rumored

It's worth being precise about where the line sits, because "Wuque Studio" and "Mammoth V2" have started showing up adjacent to each other in enough places that the adjacency risks reading as confirmation. This is a familiar pattern for any vendor that runs two or more in-house lines: once one line goes quiet for a stretch, buyers assume the studio is heads-down on the next thing rather than simply between announcements, and the guess calcifies into something that gets repeated as though it were sourced. Nothing here is meant to dismiss that guess as unreasonable — vendors with an established Mammoth and Promise catalog eventually do ship follow-ups — only to keep it clearly labeled as a guess until Wuque Studio itself says otherwise.

Confirmed: Wuque Studio published a general product-update post on July 26, 2026. Search and listing interest in the Ikki68 Aurora has picked up in the weeks since. The board itself remains available through the vendor's existing storefront, unchanged.

Not confirmed: a Mammoth V2. Not confirmed: a Promise V2. Not confirmed: any new group buy or interest check tied to either. Both follow-ups exist only as community speculation at this point — the kind of pattern-matching that happens whenever a vendor with two established lines goes quiet for a stretch and then posts anything at all. It's a reasonable guess about where Wuque might go next. It is not, as of today, a thing Wuque has said.

## What to watch

Two things separate this from a hype cycle that resolves itself. The first is whether Wuque Studio follows the July 26 post with anything more specific — a dated interest check, a configurator page, a teaser image with actual hardware in it. Until one of those appears, "Mammoth V2" and "Promise V2" stay exactly what they are today: names attached to nothing. The second is simpler and more measurable — whether the renewed Ikki68 Aurora interest holds or fades back down over the coming weeks now that the post itself is no longer fresh. A board reclaiming its reference-example status on the strength of its own reputation is a different, sturdier story than a search spike that decays once the news cycle that triggered it passes.
