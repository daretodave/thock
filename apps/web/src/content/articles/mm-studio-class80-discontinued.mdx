CannonKeys' catalog record for the MM Studio Class80 carries a status field reading discontinued, updated 2026-05-09. A status flip like that doesn't usually earn space on a news page — vendors retire boards constantly, and a TKL that first shipped four years earlier leaving the active catalog is the kind of housekeeping most readers would scroll past. But the <PartReference id="class80">Class80</PartReference>'s own catalog description already tells its life story in two clauses — R1 sold out; R2 succeeded it — and the discontinued status closes that story out without a third act. The record shows no R3, and nothing in CannonKeys' listing points toward one.

The catalog lists the Class80 as a TKL cut, CNC-machined from aluminum, with gasket mount as its headline mount style, no hot-swap sockets, and no wireless radio. It first released 2022-09-01, sold under CannonKeys — the boutique US vendor that distributes it — though <Mono>MM Studio</Mono> is the name the board itself carries as its designer. The board's own description adds the texture a bare spec row can't: retro styling, thick bezels, and a 3.5 kg unbuilt weight that put it on the heavier end of the TKL field even before a buyer added switches, stabilizers, and keycaps. None of that changed between the board's launch and its current record. The only field that moved is <Mono>status</Mono>, from whatever active state it carried before to <Mono>discontinued</Mono>.

## R1, R2, and a status field with no third act

The Class80's description compresses a multi-year run into six words: R1 sold out, R2 succeeded it. That's a clean succession story — a design launches, sells through its first run, and a second revision takes its place on the strength of the first one's reception. The catalog record doesn't carry separate dates for when R1 sold out or when R2 took over, so thock isn't reproducing timeline precision the record doesn't have. What the record does establish is the shape of the lineage: one TKL nameplate, two completed revisions, and now a status field that reads discontinued with nothing scheduled behind it. R1 handing off to R2 is a board continuing. R2 handing off to discontinued is a board stopping — at least as far as the record shows.

<PullQuote>A catalog that tracks R1 selling out and R2 taking its place is tracking a board that's still being made. A catalog that stops at discontinued is tracking one that isn't — until something in the record says otherwise.</PullQuote>

<Callout type="note" title="Discontinued isn't the same claim as sold out">
  A sold-out listing says a specific batch ran out and implies another might follow — that's what happened between the Class80's R1 and R2. A discontinued listing says the vendor isn't carrying the design forward at all, independent of whether any stock remains. The Class80's record doesn't say why the line stopped, and thock isn't inventing a supply-chain story or a vendor statement to fill that gap. The honest read is narrower: the status field changed, and no successor model has shown up in the record to explain it.
</Callout>

## The part of the Class80 worth remembering

The detail that made the Class80 a reference point in thock's own TKL coverage wasn't the retro styling — it was the mount menu. The board shipped with a buyer's choice of top mount, PCB gasket mount with a plate, or a plateless PCB gasket, three structurally different builds sold under one nameplate. thock's [mounting styles, compared](/article/mounting-styles-compared) singles the Class80 out as "one of the more studied examples of how a multi-mode mount system changes the shape of the builder's decision" — the top-mount option delivering the firm, case-resonant feel that mount style is known for, the gasket-with-plate option delivering the softer, rounder signature the market has converged on since, and the plateless PCB gasket landing somewhere looser and bouncier than either.

That flexibility also mattered to the bigger story thock traced in [how the 60% became the default custom-keyboard layout](/article/60-percent-layout-history): the Class80 was the TKL case study for gasket mount reaching the largest of the four standard footprint sizes, running the same foam-channel machining and floating-plate engineering the 60%/65%/75% family had already standardized on, rather than inventing a separate approach for the bigger chassis. A TKL buyer choosing among top mount, gasket-with-plate, and plateless PCB gasket on a single order page was choosing among three points on the exact spectrum [gasket, top-mount, tray, and integrated plate](/article/mounting-styles-compared) lays out for the whole hobby — just without having to buy three different boards to feel the difference.

## What to watch

The Class80's own description shows a design that already survived one handoff — R1 to R2 — without losing its place in the catalog. Whether this discontinuation is the same kind of pause, with an R3 eventually filling the gap the way R2 filled R1's, or a permanent exit for the nameplate, is the open question the record can't answer yet; it will answer it the moment CannonKeys or MM Studio lists a successor, or doesn't. Worth watching in parallel: whether the multi-mode mount menu the Class80 popularized at TKL size shows up on a different vendor's board now that this one has left the active catalog. The approach proved itself here — a buyer could feel the entire mount-style spectrum on one chassis — and that's a harder thing to discontinue than any single SKU.
