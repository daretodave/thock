Keychron opened a Kickstarter campaign this week for the Q16 HE 8K, a new board in its enthusiast Q-series built around a ceramic-housed TMR (tunnel magnetoresistance) magnetic switch instead of a Cherry-style mechanical contact, paired with a claimed 8000Hz polling rate. The campaign is live and picking up backers quickly, though Keychron hasn't disclosed a funding total or percentage this early, and there's no reason to guess at one.

## What launched

The Q16 HE 8K carries forward the Q-series' usual construction — aluminum case, gasket mount, the enthusiast tier thock's [look at Keychron's product ladder](/article/keychron-quiet-takeover) puts at $100 to $180 for the line generally, though Keychron hasn't confirmed final retail pricing or a full spec sheet for this specific board on the campaign page yet. What's confirmed is the switch: a magnetic-sensing design that reads stem position through a tunnel-magnetoresistance sensor rather than the Hall-effect sensor that has anchored Keychron's magnetic lineup to date, with a housing Keychron is marketing as ceramic rather than the nylon or POM blends typical of both mechanical and magnetic switches. The 8K polling claim slots the board into the same performance conversation as Keychron's wireless Q Ultra line, which [carried the same number at launch](/article/keychron-q-ultra-zmk) earlier this year.

<Callout type="note" title="What's confirmed and what isn't">
  Keychron has named the board, the ceramic-TMR switch, and the 8K polling ceiling, and the Kickstarter is live. Final pricing, shipping window, hot-swap compatibility, and reviewer-verified polling and latency numbers are not yet published. Treat the spec sheet as Keychron's stated claim until independent testing lands.
</Callout>

The Q16 HE 8K is Keychron's third distinct move in the magnetic-switch space this year, and each one has answered the sensing question differently. The Q1 HE, which reached reviewers in April 2026, committed fully to Hall-effect sensing on <Mono>ZMK</Mono> firmware — [covered at length in thock's Nova Socket piece](/article/keychron-nova-socket-hybrid), which also describes the V6 Ultra HE's "Nova Socket" hot-swap footprint, launched in July, that accepts either a standard mechanical switch or a TMR magnetic switch in the same socket, decided per key. The Q16 HE 8K skips the hedge entirely and commits the other direction: full TMR, no mechanical fallback, on a board that reads as the enthusiast Q-series' answer to the wireless Q Ultra line's Hall-effect flagship.

That's worth noting as a strategic shift, not just a spec bump. Hall-effect sensing is the technology Keychron itself did more than almost any prebuilt vendor to mainstream, a shift thock's [Hall-effect mainstreaming coverage](/article/hall-effect-mainstream) has tracked through most of 2026. TMR is a different physical read of the same magnet-and-sensor mechanism — [the distinction is covered in depth in thock's magnetic-switches explainer](/article/magnetic-switches-deep-dive) — and until September 2026, the notable TMR launch in the market belonged to someone else: Cherry's gaming sub-brand XTRFY, whose <Mono>MK Crystal TMR</Mono>-based K5 Pro Compact was, per [thock's coverage of that launch](/article/cherry-xtrfy-tmr-pivot), a hedge play from a legacy MX manufacturer trying to catch up to a category it had already lost ground in. Keychron backing TMR now, from a position of category dominance rather than catch-up, reads as a different kind of signal — a brand betting on the next sensing generation before it has to, not after falling behind on the current one.

<Callout type="note" title="Still called 'HE' on a TMR switch">
  Worth naming plainly: the Q16 HE 8K keeps the "HE" designation Keychron already established for its magnetic-switch line with the Q1 HE, even though the sensor underneath the Q16 is TMR, not Hall-effect. That's a branding choice, not a technical claim — "HE" appears to be shorthand for "magnetic switch" in Keychron's naming now, not a literal description of the sensing mechanism. Buyers comparing spec sheets should read past the model name to the switch description itself.
</Callout>

Two separate magnetic-switch stories, running at once, from the same brand; the Q16 HE 8K is the one worth a closer look here, and the V6 Ultra Hybrid pre-launch is its own story for another day.

The same snapshot is less kind to Cherry. The Cherry (MX + XTRFY) brand row sits at -25, down from -18 eight weeks earlier, and the tracker's note is direct about why: "Cherry keeps losing coverage share to magnetic and TMR rivals, with Keychron's ceramic-TMR Q16 HE and Gateron's magnetic lineup dominating this week's headlines instead." That's a pointed line for a company whose own sub-brand XTRFY was the first notable name into TMR back in July, per [thock's coverage of that pivot](/article/cherry-xtrfy-tmr-pivot). Being first into a category and losing the coverage fight to a later, better-resourced entrant is its own kind of lesson about what a launch needs to actually move a brand number: not just being early, but being the launch a news week organizes itself around.

## What to watch

Two things separate a Kickstarter announcement from a shipped product, and both apply here. The first is whether the ceramic-TMR switch's actual feel and durability claims survive contact with reviewers — a new housing material paired with a sensing technology that's barely six months old in this market is exactly the kind of combination that looks better on a campaign page than under a loupe. The second is whether the 8K polling figure holds up as a measured number rather than a best-case spec, the same gap thock flagged when Keychron's wireless line made the identical claim earlier this year. Backers deciding whether to fund the Q16 HE 8K are, in effect, betting on both questions resolving in Keychron's favor before the boards ship.
