<Mono>GMK CYL Red Devils</Mono> last ran a group buy in 2020. It's back on the thock trends tracker this week at a score of 68, direction up, off an eight-week spark trail of 10, 15, 22, 30, 40, 52, 60, 68 — a climb that hasn't flattened once since it first appeared on the board. The set reopens for orders September 14, 2026 and closes October 10, 2026, running jointly through <Mono>Omnitype</Mono> and <Mono>Keebz N Cables</Mono>, with a Q1 2027 ship estimate.

That's a notable shape for a re-run. Most second passes at a keycap set coast on nostalgia and post modestly — a bump, not a trajectory. Red Devils is doing something else: it's pulling in buyers who never saw the first run at all.

## Reading the climb

An eight-week unbroken rise is rare on its own. What's rarer is a re-run doing it. The tracker's typical second-run pattern — and this is true across GMK-tooled sets generally, not specific to any one vendor — is a short spike around the announcement, a plateau while the group-buy page sits live, and a modest close. Red Devils instead has gone 10, 15, 22, 30, 40, 52, 60, 68: every single week higher than the last, with the steepest single jump — 40 to 52 — landing in the middle of the run rather than tapering off toward it.

That shape points to two separate buyer pools arriving on two separate timelines rather than one pool reacting all at once. A pure-nostalgia re-run front-loads: people who missed it the first time and have been waiting six years hit "buy" the moment the page goes live, and the curve spikes early and flattens. Red Devils' curve does the opposite — it builds. That's consistent with a second, later-arriving audience discovering the set well into the group-buy window rather than the day it opened.

An unbroken eight-week climb isn't unique to this run this year — [GMK CYL OG Extensions](/article/gmk-cyl-og-extensions-interest-check) posted the same shape, five straight weeks up with zero flat reads, before it had even opened a group buy of its own. [DCS Olivetti](/article/dcs-olivetti-comeback) ran a comparable climb after its own multi-year gap, which makes this pattern — a return from dormancy pulling in an audience that keeps growing rather than front-loading — something the tracker has now caught more than once.

## Who that second audience is

The tracker's own note for this run points at the answer: Red Devils is drawing football-fandom crossover buyers. <Mono>GMK CYL Red Devils</Mono> is built around a club-crest colorway — the kind of keycap set that leans on a specific team's badge, kit colors, and numeral styling across its accent row and novelties, the same way GMK-tooled runs have borrowed liveries from racing teams, national flags, and retro computing brands in prior sets. The first run in 2020 would have sold almost entirely inside the keyboard hobby — people already collecting Cherry-profile sets who happened to also support the club in question.

A re-run six years later changes the math. Six years is long enough for club-focused fan communities — supporter forums, matchday Discords, fan-content Twitter and Reddit accounts — to have cycled through several seasons' worth of content and to have picked up a set like this organically through fan-side sharing rather than keyboard-hobby channels. Someone who wants a crest-colorway keycap set because they support the club, not because they already collect Cherry-profile sets, is a different kind of buyer than the ones who filled the 2020 run — and they don't discover a group buy the same way or on the same week that a keyboard-hobby regular does. That's the lag a steadily-building curve like this one would produce: nostalgia buyers showing up early, fandom buyers trickling in as the listing gets passed around supporter channels over the following weeks.

<Callout type="note">
  The tracker measures discussion volume and order-page activity, not a breakdown of who's buying. The football-fandom crossover read here comes directly from the 2026-W40 tracker note — it's the most plausible explanation for a climb this sustained, not a confirmed demographic split.
</Callout>

## What a joint Omnitype / Keebz N Cables run signals

Running the buy jointly through two vendors is itself worth noting. A single-vendor group buy is the default for most GMK CYL runs; splitting fulfillment across two storefronts is more often a sign that a vendor expects enough volume, or enough regional spread in where orders are coming from, to want two separate order pipelines rather than one. It doesn't confirm the crossover-audience read on its own, but it's consistent with it — a set expected to draw outside its usual buyer pool is exactly the kind of run a vendor might want extra fulfillment capacity for, rather than betting a single page can handle whatever shows up. [Prototypist's own tracker history](/article/prototypist-vendor-spotlight) is a useful reference point here — a vendor that's carried as many as nineteen concurrent group buys in a single week is exactly the kind of fulfillment partner a run expecting unusual volume would want alongside it.

## The honest caveats

None of this is confirmed by anything beyond the tracker's own note and the shape of eight weekly reads. There's no published breakdown of order-page traffic by referral source, and no vendor statement attributing the climb to football fandom specifically — that attribution is the tracker's read on the pattern, not a disclosed fact from Omnitype or Keebz N Cables. It's also worth flagging the obvious counter-explanation: a six-year gap is long enough that plenty of the climb could simply be hobby turnover — newer keyboard-hobby entrants who weren't collecting in 2020 discovering a well-regarded set for the first time, with no football crossover involved at all. The tracker's note leans toward the fandom explanation, but the two aren't mutually exclusive, and nothing here rules either one out.

<Callout type="warn">
  A Q1 2027 ship estimate issued before a group buy has even closed is the earliest kind of estimate a GMK-tooled run publishes — treat it as a planning anchor, not a date to build around. These estimates routinely move once tooling and plating schedules are locked in after close.
</Callout>

## What to watch

The group buy closes October 10, 2026. The number worth watching isn't just the final tracker score at close — it's whether the climb holds its shape through the last two weeks or spikes hard at the very end, the way last-minute keyboard-hobby pledges usually do. A sharp final-week jump would suggest the hobby-regular audience held off until the deadline, same as any ordinary close. A continued steady climb, instead, would be further evidence that a second, slower-moving buyer pool is still finding the page. What happens after close is worth watching too: [DCS Grass Valley's momentum transferred cleanly](/article/dcs-grass-valley-decline) to its successor the same week it closed, while [GMK CYL TA Neo](/article/gmk-cyl-ta-neo-production-tracking) cooled for three straight weeks once its own buy locked — both are the ordinary range of outcomes for a set moving from open order window to production queue, not a verdict on this one. Either way, follow the [Trends Tracker](/trends/tracker) to see the close-of-buy read, and whatever Q1 2027 ship estimate survives the production schedule between now and then.
