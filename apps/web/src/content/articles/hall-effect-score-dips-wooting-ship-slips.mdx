Eight weeks of tracker data for the Hall Effect/Rapid Trigger switch category read, in order: 98, 97, 98, 98, 99, 100, 100, 95. That last number is the one worth sitting with. It's the first down-tick the category has posted since thock started tracking it, and it arrived in the 2026-W44 snapshot — the same week Wooting's own "late October 2026" delivery promise for the 80HE+ ABS and Module tiers went a second consecutive week without public confirmation that any unit had actually shipped.

Two numbers moving in the same direction in the same week isn't proof they're connected. But the category's tracker note has been citing Wooting's roadmap, specifically, for three weeks running — and the week the note stopped being able to say "still on schedule" is the same week the score stopped climbing.

## Reading the dip against the plateau

thock argued back in August, [when the score first flattened at 97 for two straight weeks](/article/hall-effect-rapid-trigger-plateau), that a flat line at the top of a 0-100 scale isn't the same signal as a flat line in a trough. The thesis held through September and October: the score kept nudging up — 98, 98, 99 — before finally pinning the ceiling at 100 for the 2026-W42 and 2026-W43 snapshots. A category that's already converted most of the available attention has nowhere left to climb on a bounded scale, and two weeks at 100 looked like exactly that: a market that had finished winning, not one running out of steam — the same shift that's turned Hall-effect into [the mid-premium default](/article/hall-effect-mainstream) rather than a Wooting-shaped niche.

The 2026-W44 reading is the first real test of whether that read survives contact with a category whose anchor story stops being pure anticipation. Up through 2026-W43, every weekly note described a roadmap still pointing at a future date — Wooting's own target, unmet but not yet overdue. By 2026-W44, that same target had quietly become a date in the past with nothing to show for it publicly. The tracker's own language shows the shift: 2026-W43 read "no public confirmation of first units shipping yet," language that still leaves room for the delivery window to be in progress. 2026-W44 read "for a second straight week, Wooting's own timeline points to late-October delivery with no public confirmation that ABS/Module units have actually shipped" — the same fact, but now stated twice in a row, which is a different kind of fact than stating it once.

<Callout type="note" title="A tracker score is a sentiment read, not a shipping manifest">
  The Hall Effect/Rapid Trigger row measures how much of a given week's coverage, launches, and group-buy attention orbits the category relative to everything else thock tracks — it doesn't measure units in boxes. A five-point dip means coverage and buyer attention cooled slightly relative to other categories that week. It doesn't mean anyone's preorder got cancelled, and it doesn't mean Wooting missed its date by a wide margin rather than a narrow one. Those are separate questions, and only one of them is what this number answers.
</Callout>

## What was actually promised, and what's actually confirmed

[thock's own coverage of the 80HE+ preorder](/article/wooting-80he-plus-shipping-update), published September 18, 2026, laid out the split clearly: Wooting opened preorders on September 17, 2026, with ABS prebuilts and the bare Module tier slated for late October 2026, and zinc prebuilts plus the Lekker Knob Large accessory slated for mid-December 2026. That piece also flagged, at the time, that Wooting frames both windows as estimates on its own preorder page — not guaranteed dates — and that a first preorder ship date is statistically the one most likely to slip. It's a familiar pattern for Wooting specifically: [the 80HE+'s own hardware outran its firmware for months after launch](/article/wooting-rapid-trigger-head-start), so a slipping calendar date isn't the first time this product's roadmap has lagged what it shipped.

Late October 2026 has now come and gone. The tracker notes for both 2026-W43 and 2026-W44 describe the same unresolved state: Wooting's own roadmap still names that window, and no public confirmation of shipped ABS or Module units has surfaced. That's not evidence the boards didn't ship — a vendor can start fulfillment quietly, batch by batch, well before anyone posts about it publicly, and "no public confirmation" is a statement about visibility, not about warehouses. But it's also not evidence they did. The category's attention, this round, is being measured against a date Wooting set for itself, and that date is the thing that moved first.

The 80HE+ is notable mostly because of what it isn't: a new sensing architecture or a new pitch, but an upgrade pass on a board that was already Wooting's volume seller, replacing the discontinued original 80HE with an FR4 plate, <Mono>Lekker Tikken Medium</Mono> hotswap switches, a split-spacebar layout on the Module tier, and 8kHz <Mono>Tachyon Mode</Mono> polling. None of that changes the sensing story Wooting has told since the <PartReference id="wooting-60he">Wooting 60HE</PartReference> shipped in April 2022 with hotswappable Lekker Hall-effect switches and per-key adjustable rapid trigger — reviewers still measure every HE or TMR board that's launched since against that reference point. The 80HE+ was never the category's technical news. It was the category's biggest open calendar item, which is exactly why a slip on it, rather than a slip on some smaller vendor's side project, is the kind of thing that could move a tracker score that's otherwise been running on pure momentum. It's also the clearest case study in [Rapid Trigger's crossover from enthusiast curiosity to gaming-board default](/article/rapid-trigger-gaming-crossover) — the hardware Wooting is shipping late is the same hardware the rest of the category has already normalized.

## One data point isn't a trend reversal

A single down-tick after eight weeks of climb and plateau is worth flagging, not worth overreading. The [2026-W44 Trends Tracker](/trends/tracker/2026-W44) snapshot puts the category at 95 — still a dominant score by any absolute reading, five points off its ceiling, and comfortably ahead of where it sat in midsummer. Nothing about a 95 says the category lost the room, and Hall-effect isn't the only row worth watching: [TMR has closed five straight weeks of ground on Hall-effect's lead](/article/tmr-switches-five-week-climb) without catching it yet, which is a different kind of pressure on the same ceiling. What it says is that the story holding the score up has, for the first time, started to carry a small cost for going unresolved past its own deadline.

What would turn this into something more than a one-week wobble: another down-tick in the 2026-W45 snapshot without a shipping confirmation from Wooting, or — on the other side — a confirmation post that resolves the question outright and lets the score either recover toward 100 or settle somewhere lower on its own merits rather than on uncertainty. Either way, the next couple of tracker weeks are a cleaner test of the plateau thesis than the plateau itself ever was. A flat score at the ceiling was easy to read as a market that had finished converting. A score that moves, for a reason this specific, is the first data point that actually says something new.
