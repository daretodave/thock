thock reads the mechanical-keyboard hobby the way its builders do — switch by switch, layout by layout, group buy by group buy. The weekly tracker is the editorial spine behind that reading: a scored, directional snapshot refreshed every Monday. This issue rounds up five pieces worth the read, one per pillar, then closes with the tracker's clearest signals from the week of August 3, 2026.

## Guides — the fork wired firmware doesn't prepare you for

**[ZMK for wireless and split builds: a practical guide](/article/zmk-wireless-split-firmware-guide)**

QMK gets a builder through almost any wired board without a second thought, but that confidence doesn't carry over once a battery and a second half enter the picture. The piece lays out why: ZMK was built on Zephyr from its first commit with Bluetooth and split pairing as day-one assumptions, not features grafted onto a wired codebase later. It also walks the honest gap in ZMK Studio's GUI — where it covers key reassignment cleanly and where a hand-edited devicetree file is still unavoidable.

Read it if you're speccing a split or wireless board and were about to default to QMK-plus-VIA out of habit.

## Ideas — three zones, not the whole board

**[Mixing keycap profiles on one board: what actually works](/article/mixing-keycap-profiles)**

The piece narrows down where a profile mix survives contact with actual use: a single accent key, the modifier row, and the spacebar — never the alpha block, where sculpted row-to-row geometry breaks the moment a mismatched cap interrupts it. It also flags the failure mode nobody photographs for a build thread until months later: an ABS accent cap developing shine against a PBT base long after the swap looked color-matched on install day.

Read it before dropping a spare SA cap onto a Cherry-profile Esc key without knowing which failure mode you're risking.

## Trends — what a closed group buy's cooldown actually means

**[GMK CYL TA Neo's group buy is closed — here's what a November 2026 ship estimate actually means](/article/gmk-cyl-ta-neo-production-tracking)**

<Mono>GMK CYL TA Neo</Mono> closed its buy July 17, 2026, and its tracker score has fallen for two straight snapshots since — 50, then 40. The piece argues that's the wrong thing to read as a warning: once ordering decisions are locked, the forum chatter that drove the score dries up by design, not because interest evaporated. The sharper point is relative — TA Neo's score gets crowded out week over week by <Mono>GMK CYL OG Extensions</Mono>, a still-open buy pulling the community's attention while it lasts.

Read it if you've ever watched a set you already bought into "lose" tracker ground and wondered whether that meant something.

## News — a household name enters the enthusiast supply chain

**[OpenAI's first hardware is a Work Louder macro pad](/article/work-louder-openai-codex-micro)**

Codex Micro, OpenAI's first branded hardware, is built by boutique vendor Work Louder on the bones of its $199 <Mono>Creator Micro 2</Mono>. It launched July 15, 2026 at $230 and sold out within 24 hours, with resale listings now clearing $700-800+. The piece frames the real story as the choice of partner — OpenAI went to an existing boutique platform instead of a faceless contract manufacturer, borrowing Work Louder's credibility with an audience that already cares about per-key programmability.

Read it if you want the case for why a software company's first hardware move landed inside the enthusiast supply chain instead of around it.

## Deep dive — the geekhack thread behind a decade of vocabulary

**[How the 60% became the default custom-keyboard layout](/article/60-percent-layout-history)**

Every buyer's guide opens with the same four-term taxonomy — 60%, 65%, 75%, TKL — as though it always existed. The piece traces it to one open-source PCB out of a geekhack thread, and works through why that single case-and-plate tooling decision still shapes every compact layout built since.

Read it if the 60%/65%/75% vocabulary has always felt assumed rather than explained.
