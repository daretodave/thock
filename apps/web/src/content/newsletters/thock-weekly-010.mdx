## Guides — read the layout before the keycap set ships

**[ANSI, ISO, or JIS: a practical layout buying guide](/article/ansi-iso-jis-keyboard-layout-guide)**

<Mono>ANSI</Mono>, <Mono>ISO</Mono>, and <Mono>JIS</Mono> describe key geometry, not language or legends, and a listing photo rarely spells out which one a board uses. The piece walks the Enter key shape, the Left Shift width, and the bottom-row unit math that determines whether a keycap set clicks into place or leaves a gap — the kind of spec-sheet shorthand worth decoding before an order ships, not after.

Read it if you've ever ordered a keycap set by eye and found out the hard way it was cut for the wrong layout.

## Ideas — a split board is two decisions, not one

**[Parts pairing for a split build: two independent halves, two independent decisions](/article/split-ergo-parts-pairing)**

A split keyboard is really two independent keyboards sharing a keymap, but most builders still shop for it as if it were one — one switch order, one sculpted keycap set, one tuning applied evenly across both halves. The piece makes the case for treating each half on its own terms, down to running different switches or different lube weights left to right where the halves actually see different use.

Read it if you've built a split board and never once considered tuning the two halves differently.

## Deep dive — damping catches the stem twice, not once

**[What a silent switch actually silences — and what it can't](/article/silent-switch-damping-deep-dive)**

A keystroke lands twice — once on the downstroke floor, once on the upstroke ceiling — and Cherry's molded stem pads and Gazzew's silicone pads exist to catch both impacts. The piece uses the undamped <Mono>Boba U4T</Mono> as a control to isolate what damping is actually buying, about 14 dB on one tester's meter, and lays out the tradeoffs in travel and rebound that come with muting a bump that still has to land somewhere.

Read it if you've assumed "silent" switches are just quieter versions of their standard counterparts rather than a different mechanical compromise.
