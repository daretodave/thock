## Guides — flip the cap over before you buy

**[Artisan keycaps: a practical buying guide](/article/artisan-keycaps-buying-guide)**

An artisan keycap is a single collectible cap, usually cast resin and occasionally ceramic or 3D-printed, bought through raffles or blind boxes rather than added to a cart like a normal keycap set. Pricing roughly sorts into three bands — entry, mid, and grail-tier — but the one check that prevents the most common beginner mistake costs nothing: flip the cap over and confirm its stem type matches the board. An <Mono>MX</Mono> stem on an <Mono>Alps</Mono> board, or either on a low-profile board, simply won't seat.

Read it if you've ever bid on a cap without checking whether it fits anything you own.

## Deep dive — what a tunnel junction actually measures

**[What a TMR sensor is actually measuring under a magnetic switch](/article/tmr-switches-deep-dive)**

A TMR sensor reads the resistance across a magnetic tunnel junction — two ferromagnetic layers separated by an insulating barrier a few nanometers thick — rather than a Hall sensor's direct voltage reading. Per <Mono>DFRobot</Mono>'s figures, that's roughly a two-thousand-fold sensitivity advantage and a lower noise floor at the component level, which matters most at the shallow, weak-field end of a keystroke, exactly where rapid-trigger firmware is most prone to false double-fires. <Mono>Wooting</Mono>, a Hall-effect maker, published a direct rebuttal arguing the real-world gap has mostly closed with careful calibration. The piece lands between both camps' marketing.

Read it if you want the mechanism underneath this issue's Trends pick, not just the chart.
